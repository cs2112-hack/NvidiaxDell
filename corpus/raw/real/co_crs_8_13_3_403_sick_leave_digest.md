# Colorado paid sick leave — numeric rules (digest)

Paraphrased. Fetch the verbatim section from the Colorado Revised Statutes before any
external use; the accrual ratio and caps below are the operative facts.

- **Accrual rate:** one hour of paid sick leave for every thirty (30) hours worked.
- **Annual accrual/use cap:** forty-eight (48) hours per year. An employer is not
  required to allow accrual or use beyond 48 hours in a year.
- **Carryover:** accrued unused leave carries over to the following year, subject to
  the same 48-hour annual use cap.
- **Availability:** leave is available for use as it is accrued (no 90-day waiting
  period, unlike California and Washington).
- **Public health emergency supplement:** on declaration of a public health emergency,
  employees are entitled to additional paid leave (up to 80 hours for a 40-hour-per-week
  employee), reduced by leave already available under the accrual rule.

**Relevance to this corpus:** the 1:30 accrual ratio and the 48-hour cap are a
statutory floor that conflicts with the Acme handbook's 1:40 accrual and 40-hour cap
(SYN-005 § 4.2) for any employee working in Colorado.

# Colorado final paycheck — numeric rules (digest)

Paraphrased from Colo. Rev. Stat. § 8-4-109.

- **Involuntary termination:** wages are due **immediately**. If the employer's payroll
  unit is not scheduled to operate at the time of separation, payment is due within six
  (6) hours after the start of the next workday, or within twenty-four (24) hours if the
  payroll unit is offsite.
- **Employee quits:** wages are due on the next regular payday.
- **Vacation pay:** earned and determinable vacation pay is wages and must be paid on
  separation; an agreement to forfeit it is void.

**Relevance to this corpus:** conflicts with the Acme handbook's "next regular payroll
date" rule (SYN-005 § 9.3) for involuntary terminations in Colorado, and with § 9.4 (no
PTO payout) for any Colorado separation.
