# Marchetti v Northbank Systems Ltd [2026] EAT 118

**Employment Appeal Tribunal. Judgment handed down 4 August 2026.**

## Facts

The claimant was employed as a shift supervisor at job grade 6. Her contract
incorporated a working time annex in materially the same terms as many in the
sector: a general overtime entitlement at 1.25 times base rate for hours in
excess of 40; a provision that an employee of grade 5 or above "is not entitled
to an overtime payment" but accrues time off in lieu; and a precedence
provision stating that where two or more provisions would apply to the same
hour, "only the single highest applicable multiplier applies to that hour".

Over fourteen months she worked 611 hours in excess of 40 per week. She was
paid nothing for them and was credited with time off in lieu, 340 hours of
which she was never able to take. She claimed unlawful deduction from wages.

## Holding

The appeal is allowed.

1. A precedence provision expressed in terms of which provision "applies" to an
   hour operates on its own words. Where one provision would confer a
   multiplier and another would confer none, the provision conferring the
   higher multiplier is the one that applies. The employer's construction —
   that a provision introduced by the words "by way of exception" is thereby
   removed from the precedence rule's scope altogether — requires words to be
   read into the contract which are not there.

2. Where a contract is capable of two constructions and the employer drafted
   it, the construction less favourable to the employer is to be preferred.
   That principle is not displaced by the fact that the competing construction
   would render another provision of the same contract of narrower effect.

3. It follows that for each hour in excess of 40 the claimant was entitled to
   payment at the rate the general overtime provision confers, notwithstanding
   the grade exclusion, and that a substitution of time off in lieu for that
   payment was a deduction from wages within the meaning of section 13.

4. Nothing in this judgment prevents an employer from excluding senior
   employees from overtime. It requires only that the exclusion be drafted so
   that the precedence provision does not defeat it — by confining the
   precedence provision to the multipliers it is intended to rank, or by
   stating expressly that the exclusion operates notwithstanding it.

## Disposal

Remitted for calculation. The tribunal is directed to compute the sums due at
the general overtime rate for hours between 40 and 48, and at the higher
overtime rate for hours in excess of 48, for the whole of the period of
employment, subject to the statutory limitation period.
